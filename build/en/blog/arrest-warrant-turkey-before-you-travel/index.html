Will I Be Arrested When I Enter Turkey? Arrest Warrants Explained (2026)

Av. Hasan Doğru
9 September 2026
10 min read

Whether an arrest warrant exists in Turkey can be checked before you travel — and in many cases resolved. This guide shows which record shows what, why e-Devlet hides the warrant, and which steps are possible from abroad.

  • e-Devlet, criminal record, GBT: which record shows an arrest warrant — and which does not?
  • Checking only via the UYAP Lawyer Portal: why the lawyer-run query is the only reliable route
  • İstinabe, objection, GBT delay: what can be resolved before entering Turkey

Av. Hasan Doğru · Turkish criminal law · Mannheim & Ankara

Legal notice: This article addresses Turkish law exclusively and is provided for general information. Doğru Kanzlei advises on Turkish law under § 207 BRAO and does not advise on German domestic law.
You have lived abroad for years — in Germany, the Netherlands, the UK, Sweden, wherever home now is. You have heard, or you only suspect, that there is a case against you in Turkey. Maybe an old business dispute. Maybe a social media post. Maybe a complaint you never knew was filed.

And now there is a wedding, a funeral, or you simply want to see your parents. But you don't know what happens when you hand your passport to the officer at the airport. That uncertainty keeps people away from Turkey for years.

In most cases, the risk can be checked in advance — and in most cases it can be resolved. This guide explains which system shows what, which system hides the thing you actually need to see, and what concrete steps are possible before you fly.

What actually happens at the border

Everyone who enters Turkey — at an airport or a land crossing — is checked against the GBT system (Genel Bilgi Toplama, "general information gathering") during passport control. This check is automatic and applies to everyone.

GBT pools data from the police, the gendarmerie, the coast guard and the judicial authorities. If there is an arrest warrant against you, the record appears immediately, and you are detained and taken before the nearest judicial authority.

But there is a distinction most people don't know: not every arrest warrant means detention. Some Turkish arrest warrants exist only to take a statement; after you testify, you are released. Others can turn into pre-trial detention. What decides the outcome is the content of the file and the nature of the accusation. Of two people on the same flight, one can be released in two hours and the other detained.

⚠️ Important: Do not "try your luck" at the airport. The moment a detention record shows on screen, the initiative passes entirely to the border police. Your lawyer can only step in once you are already in custody.

Three separate records people constantly confuse

  • Criminal record certificate (Adli Sicil Kaydı): Shows only final convictions. It does not show an ongoing investigation, an open case, or an arrest warrant.
  • Pending case files: Visible through the UYAP Citizen Portal (accessed via e-Devlet), but only files where you are a party and there is no confidentiality order.
  • Arrest warrant: Entered only into the GBT system used by law enforcement. Citizens have no direct online access to this system.

    • The critical point: an arrest warrant from the investigation stage is never visible on e-Devlet or the UYAP Citizen Portal. So logging into e-Devlet and concluding "nothing shows up, so I'm clean" is a dangerous mistake. The riskiest files are precisely the ones you cannot see there.

    So how do you actually find out?

    The only reliable route: give a power of attorney to a lawyer admitted in Turkey, and have that lawyer check the UYAP Lawyer Portal.

    The legal basis is Article 153 of the Turkish Code of Criminal Procedure (CMK): defence counsel is entitled to examine the file during the investigation stage and take copies. Through the Lawyer Portal, counsel can also see entries that are closed to citizen access: the status of investigation files, the stage of any case, final decisions, enforcement details, and the existence and scope of any arrest warrant.

    This power has one limit. Under Article 153(2) CMK, for certain listed ("catalogue") offences, file access can be restricted by a judge on the prosecutor's request where disclosure would endanger the purpose of the investigation. But that restriction does not apply to statement records or expert reports.

    For a thorough check, looking at UYAP alone is sometimes not enough; where necessary, the lawyer also inspects the file in person at the registry of the relevant courthouse. You do not need to travel to Turkey for this — the process mirrors the one in defending a Turkish criminal case from abroad.

    If there is a warrant — what can be done before you travel

  • Route 1 — Challenge the warrant. Your lawyer can file an objection with the prosecutor's office or the court that issued it. If the objection succeeds, the warrant is lifted.
  • Route 2 — Testimony by letter of request (İstinabe). For people living abroad, the most effective route. Your lawyer petitions the court to take your statement by rogatory letter, stating that you have a settled centre of life abroad and there is no flight risk. Through this route the warrant can be lifted or narrowed to "for the purpose of testimony." Your statement can be taken from your country of residence, through the consulate or the SEGBİS video system.
  • Route 3 — Conditional lifting. In some cases the court lifts the warrant on conditions: giving a statement, confirming an address, or accepting judicial control measures.
  • Route 4 — The warrant lapses by law. An arrest warrant falls away when, among other things: the suspect's statement has been taken; a decision of non-prosecution (KYOK) is issued; an acquittal becomes final; or the prosecution or sentence becomes time-barred under Articles 66–68 of the Turkish Penal Code.
    • ⚠️ Important: Do not calculate the limitation period yourself. It depends on the type of offence and on events that interrupt or suspend it. Acting on "it's been ten years anyway" can cause serious, hard-to-repair consequences.

    The practical detail no one mentions: the GBT delay

    After an arrest warrant is lifted, removing the record from the GBT system can take a few hours to several days. In practice this means: your lawyer has had the warrant lifted, you are holding the court decision — but if you board a flight immediately, you can still be stopped at the border, because the system has not been updated yet.

    Recommendation: wait a few days after the warrant is lifted, and just before travelling, have your lawyer confirm that the GBT record has cleared. Carry a certified copy of the decision lifting the warrant (with the UYAP barcode, or an original).

    The case types most common in the diaspora

  • Fraud and aggravated fraud allegations — especially from former business relationships
  • Cases based on cheques and promissory notes
  • Investigations opened over social media posts
  • Cases connected to military service obligations
  • Complaints from family disputes (threats, insult, maintenance)
  • Old traffic or tax enforcement matters

    • One point to watch: courts and prosecutors sometimes issue a warrant specifically because the suspect lives abroad, treating that as a high flight risk. That is why the "settled life" evidence in the letter-of-request petition — registration, employment, tenancy, children's schooling — matters so much.

    Other consequences, and the question of service

    An arrest warrant does not only cause problems at the border. It can also prevent passport transactions being processed in Turkey, affect your consular business, and — if a Red Notice has been issued depending on the file — mean you are wanted internationally through Interpol. A warrant issued in Turkey is not directly enforceable in your country of residence; an Interpol Red Notice changes that.

    Most of our clients say the same thing: "I never knew." The reason is usually service of process. Turkish Notification Law No. 7201 has special rules for notifying citizens who live abroad. Service to your old address in Turkey can be treated as valid even if you no longer live there. If the service can be shown to be defective, the time limits restart and the decision can be challenged.
